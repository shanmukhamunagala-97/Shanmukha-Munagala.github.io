Years of Spiritual Awareness

I didn't set out to be a teacher. I set out to understand people and make children understand — why we struggle, why we light up, why some knowledge transforms lives while other knowledge simply sits on a shelf. Teaching found me, and I found my soul's journey inside it.

"Where inquiry meets empathy, and learning becomes a way of being."

I am Shanmukha, an educator driven by inquiry, research, and meaningful learning experiences. My work spans facilitating Global Perspectives (Cambridge, Grades 8–12) and transitioning into Individuals and Societies (IB MYP, Grades 6–8). Regardless of the curriculum, I focus on building inquiry skills, research thinking, and project-based learning grounded in real-world contexts.

Over the past four years in education, I have come to deeply understand the importance of exposing children to diverse models of learning — varied pedagogies, curricula, skills, and subjects. When these experiences are combined with attention to social-emotional, physical, mental, and spiritual well-being — especially in the early years up to Grade 10 — they nurture lifelong learners who are open to growth, exploration, and self-discovery.

Before stepping into formal teaching, I pursued a B.Tech in Aerospace Engineering. Those years sparked a deeper philosophical inquiry into life, childhood, and the inner child within each of us. This curiosity led me to the Gandhi Fellowship, where I worked with tribal children, families, and communities in Singrauli, Madhya Pradesh — an experience that grounded me in local realities and prompted me to question the effectiveness of mainstream educational policies.

Seeking deeper understanding, I pursued a Master of Arts in Education from Azim Premji University. Over nearly a decade of reflection and exploration, I have come to see life itself as a school — and each of us as learners on a continuous journey.

I am deeply invested in the well-being of children, adults, and the larger ecosystem. I believe in nurturing the five dimensions of being: physical, intellectual, emotional, social, and spiritual. At the core, I value love, awareness, and conscious living — choosing understanding over ego, and connection over individualism.

My Philosophy

Four beliefs that hold everything together

01

Earth is a School

All beings come to Earth for learning and we learn from our birth to our last breath. Our planet is not just made of five elements — Air, Water, Soil, Fire, Space — it also contains the profound wisdom these elements and our souls bring. That's why Earth is not just a living planet, it's a learning planet. And that's why we all are lifelong learners.

02

School is for Life

Children learn from spaces they trust. Before any curriculum, we must invest deeply in the quality of connection — with students, parents, and communities. Safety is the soil from which genuine curiosity grows, and lays the foundation for children to trust themselves, build confidence, and excel in the path they take.

03

Living Questions

Where there is freedom to ask questions, there is freedom to think deeper and make meaningful connections. I am suspicious of teachers who only give answers. The most profound education happens when we model genuine uncertainty — when we show children that not-knowing is not a failure, but the beginning of thinking.

04

Meaningful Wholeness

Open-ended thinking, when supported by a healthy and nurturing environment, fosters meaningful connections. Students begin to see subjects, activities, sports, arts, labs, and all experiences not as separate entities, but as interconnected parts of a whole — a wholesome "meal" that nourishes all dimensions of child growth.

Young Researcher's Program

A thoughtfully designed introduction to research for students in Grades 8–12. It empowers learners to move beyond rote learning and develop critical thinking, inquiry, research, and effective communication — and most importantly, a love for knowledge and research.

Students learn how to ask meaningful questions, investigate real-world topics, and present ideas with clarity. The program bridges CBSE/ICSE and international approaches (IB/Cambridge) by fostering independent thinking and application-based learning. Students create their own research report and presentation by the end.
